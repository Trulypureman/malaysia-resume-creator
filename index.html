Both templates are ATS-safe: single column, real text and standard headings. Reorder sections with ⠿ or ↑ ↓, and your resume follows the same order.

Off by default. ATS doesn't need these fields, and putting your NRIC on a resume is an identity-fraud risk. Turn this on only if the employer asks, such as some government agencies, GLCs or SMEs. Most will collect these details on their own application form.

Aim for 40–100 words. Reuse the exact job title and 2–3 key skills from the job ad, because ATS ranks matches near the top highly. Don't write "I" or use buzzwords like "hardworking".
List 8–15 skills, mostly hard skills, named exactly as the job ad does: "Google Analytics 4", not "analytics". Abbreviations: add both if you have room, e.g. "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)".
Formula: Action verb + what you did + measurable result, e.g. "Reduced invoice processing time 40% by automating approvals in SAP." Put numbers in at least 40% of your bullets.
Have a degree? Leave SPM out unless the job asks for it. Include your CGPA if it is 3.00 or higher, or if you graduated within the last 3 years.
Disclosing your current salary is optional and it weakens your negotiating position. Many recruiters ask for expected salary only, and JobStreet collects it on the application form anyway.
Most recruiters assume references are available, so leaving this off saves space. Turn it on if your target employer expects the line. Always ask your referees for permission first.
